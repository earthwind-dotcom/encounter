There's a well-known story about blind men touching an elephant. One feels the trunk and says
it's a snake. One feels the leg and says it's a tree. The point is usually: religions are all
touching the same reality from different sides. Nobody's wrong, nobody's fully right.

It's a generous instinct. It's worth being generous back and taking it seriously.

## What's true in it

The world's religions share a great deal of hard-won wisdom: humility, compassion, honesty,
care for the poor, the sense that we're not the center of everything. Christians don't need to
deny that. Paul, speaking in Athens, quoted Greek poets approvingly (Acts 17:28). Truth is
truth wherever you find it.

## Where it doesn't hold

If you ask the religions themselves, most don't think they're saying the same thing.

- Buddhism, in most of its forms, doesn't teach a personal creator God. Christianity, Judaism
  and Islam do.
- Islam honors Jesus as a prophet but says he wasn't crucified (Qur'an 4:157). Christianity says
  his crucifixion and resurrection are the center of everything.
- Hinduism has many paths and many gods. Judaism insists there is one God and only one.

These aren't small differences in emphasis. Saying they're all the same can end up quietly
disrespecting each of them, by ignoring what their followers actually believe. And notice: the
elephant story only works because the storyteller can see the whole elephant. That's a big
claim too.

## Christianity's particular claim

Christianity doesn't mainly claim to have better advice. It claims something happened: that God
came in person, in a specific man, in a specific place, and was executed and raised. Jesus said,
"I am the way, the truth, and the life" (John 14:6). That's narrow. It's either true or it
isn't, and it's worth checking.

## What about people who never heard?

That's a real and fair question, and Christians don't all answer it the same way. Some believe
explicit faith in Jesus is necessary. Many, including the official teaching of the Catholic
Church since the 1960s, hold that God can reach people through Christ even when they don't
know his name. C. S. Lewis pictured a sincere follower of a false god being welcomed by the
true one, who tells him that the good he did in that god's name was really done for him.

What Christians agree on is that God is more just and more merciful than we are. You don't have
to have everyone else's fate worked out.

## Where this leaves you

The question for you isn't really about everyone else. It's about Jesus, and what you make of
him. If his claim is true, it matters. If it isn't, it doesn't. Either way, it's worth reading
what he said before deciding he was saying the same thing as everyone else.
