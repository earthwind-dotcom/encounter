Maybe you're drawn to Jesus but stuck on the Bible. The talking snake. The flood. The
genealogies where everyone lives nine hundred years. You've been told it's all or nothing:
either you believe every word literally, or you're not really a Christian.

That's not how it works, and it's never been how it worked.

## Jesus came first

The first followers of Jesus followed a person. They'd met him, or met people who'd met him.
Paul's letters, the earliest Christian writings, weren't written until about twenty years
after the crucifixion. The Gospels came later. The New Testament as a collection took shape
over the next few centuries.

For those first decades, people weren't following a book. They were following Jesus, and the
book grew out of their witness to him.

## Jesus is the Word. The Bible points to him.

John's Gospel begins: "In the beginning was the Word... and the Word became flesh and lived
among us" (John 1:1, 14). The "Word of God," in John, is a person: Jesus himself.

Christians call the Bible God's word too, because it's the witness that points to him. The
theologian Karl Barth described it as a threefold word: Jesus, the Word in person; the Bible,
which witnesses to him; and preaching, which passes that witness on. Jesus is at the center.

What this means for you: the center of Christianity isn't a book you have to defend. It's a
person you can meet.

## Serious, not literal

Taking the Bible seriously means reading each part as the kind of writing it is. Psalms are
poems. Proverbs are proverbs, not promises. Parables are true without being events. Genesis 1
is a carefully built poem about who made the world and why. Revelation is coded resistance
literature written to persecuted churches, not a newspaper from the future.

"Literal" often just means "flat," and flat readings miss what the writers were actually
doing. It's possible to take the Bible more seriously, not less, by refusing to flatten it.

## Christians disagree about the details

Some Christians hold that the Bible is without error in everything it affirms. Others hold that
God speaks through fully human writers, with their languages, cultures, and limits, the same
way God came to us in Jesus, fully human. Both views are held by people who love Jesus and
read the Bible carefully. You don't have to decide between them today.

## Where this leaves you

Start with Jesus. Read one of the Gospels (Mark is the shortest) and ask, "Who is this?" You'll
have questions about the rest of the Bible, and they're good questions. They can come along
with you. They don't have to be settled before you begin.
