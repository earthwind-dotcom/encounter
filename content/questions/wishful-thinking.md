Freud's version is the famous one: we're scared and small, so we invent a big father in the sky
to comfort us. Religion is a projection of what we wish were true.

It's a fair challenge, and it's worth taking seriously, including about yourself. People do
believe things because they want them to be true. Christians included.

## It cuts both ways

If wanting God to exist makes belief suspicious, then not wanting God to exist makes unbelief
suspicious too. And plenty of people have that wish. The philosopher Thomas Nagel, an atheist,
was unusually frank about it: he wrote that he hoped there was no God, and didn't want the
universe to be like that.

The point isn't that anyone's motives are bad. It's that motives don't settle the question.
Everyone has them. You still have to look at the evidence.

## Where a belief comes from isn't whether it's true

You might believe the earth goes around the sun because your teacher said so. That's not a
great reason. It's still true. Explaining *why* someone believes something doesn't show
whether it's *true*. Philosophers call that mistake the genetic fallacy.

So the real question isn't "could faith be wishful?" It's "is there good reason to think it's
true?" That's what the rest of this site is trying to help you weigh.

## This isn't what anyone would wish for

Look at what Christianity actually says, and ask whether it's what people invent to feel good.

- A messiah who was tortured to death in public, the most shameful way to die.
- "Love your enemies. Pray for those who persecute you."
- "Take up your cross and follow me."
- A God who knows everything about you, and to whom you'll give an account.

If you were designing a comforting religion from scratch, this probably isn't it. The first
people to believe it weren't comforted into it. Many of them lost everything for it.

## Wanting something isn't proof it isn't there

C. S. Lewis noticed that our deepest longings usually correspond to something real. Hunger
points to food; thirst to water. He suggested that if we find in ourselves a longing nothing in
this world satisfies, one explanation is that we were made for something beyond it. That isn't
proof. But the longing you feel isn't automatically suspicious either.

## Where this leaves you

Be honest with yourself about what you want to be true, in either direction. Then look at the
evidence anyway. If you want somewhere to start, read the account of the resurrection in 1
Corinthians 15, then [what historians make of it](/questions/resurrection).
