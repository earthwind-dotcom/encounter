Ask most people what Christianity is about and you'll hear some version of this: be good,
believe the right things, go to church, and when you die you'll go to heaven. Be bad, and you
won't.

If that's what you think Christianity is, it makes sense that you'd walk away from it. It's a
bit exhausting, a bit smug, and not very different from every other self-improvement plan.

It's also not what Jesus taught.

## What Jesus actually announced

Jesus' own one-line summary was this: "The time has come. The kingdom of God has come near.
Turn around, and trust this good news" (Mark 1:15, our plain paraphrase).

"Kingdom of God" doesn't mean a place you go when you die. It means God's rule, God putting
the world right, arriving here. The prayer Jesus taught his followers asks for it plainly:
"your kingdom come, your will be done, on earth as it is in heaven." The direction of travel
is heaven coming to earth, not people escaping from it.

## Who it was for

Watch who Jesus spent time with. Tax collectors (collaborators, basically). People everyone
knew had messed up their lives. The sick, the poor, outsiders. The religious people of his day
were scandalized. He said: "I didn't come for the people who think they're fine. I came for
the ones who know they're not" (Mark 2:17, our paraphrase).

That's the opposite of "be good enough and you're in." It's "come as you are, and I'll start
changing you."

## So what is it, actually?

Here's the core, in plain words:

1. **You were made for connection** with God, with other people, and with your own life.
2. **Something's broken**, in the world and in us. The old word is "sin." Plainly, it's the pull
   to run our own lives our own way, and the damage that follows.
3. **God didn't stay at a distance.** In Jesus, God came close. At the cross he took the
   wreckage on himself instead of leaving it on us. The resurrection is the sign it worked.
4. **The offer is a gift.** The old word is "grace." You don't clean yourself up first. You
   can't earn it and you don't have to.
5. **The response is trust.** Turn toward him, lean your weight on him, and start following.
   He does the changing, over time, with other people around you.

Being good matters. But in Christianity, goodness is what grows *after* you've been accepted,
not the price of being accepted. That order changes everything.

## Where this leaves you

If you rejected Christianity because it looked like a moral performance, you may have
rejected something Jesus would have rejected too. The real thing is stranger and kinder: an
invitation to a person, not a program.

You can say yes to that invitation whenever you're ready, in your own words. You can also say
"not yet" and keep looking. Both are honest answers. If you want to see what following him
might look like, [The Course](/course) walks through it one question at a time.
