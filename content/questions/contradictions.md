Someone sends you a list: "101 Bible contradictions." Some items on it are silly. Some aren't.
And if you grew up being told the Bible is perfect in every detail, even one real one can feel
like the whole thing falls apart.

So let's be straight about it.

## Yes, there are real differences

A few examples you can check yourself:

- **How Judas died.** Matthew says he hanged himself (Matthew 27:5). Acts says he fell and his
  body burst open (Acts 1:18).
- **Who was at the tomb.** Mark has three women. John focuses on Mary Magdalene alone.
- **The day Jesus died.** In Mark, Matthew and Luke, the Last Supper is the Passover meal. In
  John, Jesus dies on the afternoon before Passover, as the lambs are being slaughtered (John
  19:14).
- **Who prompted David's census.** In 2 Samuel 24:1, it's God. When the same story is retold in
  1 Chronicles 21:1, it's Satan.

People have proposed ways to reconcile some of these. Some of those proposals work. Some are
a stretch. It's fine to say so.

## They kept them on purpose

Here's what's worth noticing. Around the year 170, a Christian named Tatian blended the four
Gospels into one smooth story with the differences ironed out. It was popular for a while. The
church as a whole went the other way: it kept four separate Gospels, differences and all, side
by side.

The same thing happens in the Old Testament. Chronicles retells Samuel and Kings, and changes
things. Genesis puts two creation accounts next to each other (Genesis 1 and Genesis 2) that
tell the story from different angles. Ruth and Jonah push back against other books about
foreigners. Nobody hid any of this. It was all left in plain sight.

## What kind of book does that?

Not a legal document. Not a science textbook. A library, written by many people over about a
thousand years, where different witnesses tell the story their own way for their own
audience. Ancient biographers regularly compressed events, moved them around, and framed them
to make a point. Modern readers call that a contradiction. Ancient readers called it telling
the story well.

That doesn't make every difference comfortable. It does mean the question "is the Bible
perfectly uniform?" was never the right question. The better one is "what is each of these
writers trying to show me?"

## Why this doesn't sink faith

Christians don't believe in the Bible first and Jesus second. The first Christians followed
Jesus for decades before the New Testament was even written. The Bible is a witness pointing
to him. Martin Luther compared the Bible to the manger Christ was laid in: rough wood and
swaddling cloths, holding something far more precious than itself. A manger can have
scratches on it.

If your faith was built on "every word must line up perfectly," a real contradiction feels
like an earthquake. If it's built on a person the witnesses point to, you can read the
differences with curiosity instead of fear.

## Where this leaves you

You don't have to defend the Bible to take Jesus seriously. You can read it honestly, notice
what doesn't line up, and still find yourself facing him. Try reading the four accounts of
Easter morning side by side (Mark 16, Matthew 28, Luke 24, John 20). Notice the differences.
Then notice what all four insist on anyway.
