The story most of us absorbed goes something like this: religion used to explain the world,
then science came along and explained it better, and religion has been retreating ever since.
Galileo was persecuted, Darwin won, and faith is what's left for things we haven't figured
out yet.

It's a tidy story. Historians who actually study this period mostly say it isn't true.

## The history is more interesting than the myth

Many of the people who built modern science were devout: Kepler, Boyle, Newton, Faraday,
Maxwell, and Gregor Mendel, the monk who founded genetics. Many of them saw studying nature
as a way of honoring the one who made it. The Galileo affair was real, and the church handled
it badly, but it was tangled up with politics, personalities, and the science of the day, not a
simple clash of faith against facts.

Today, plenty of serious scientists follow Jesus. Francis Collins, who led the project that
mapped the human genome, is one well-known example.

## Genesis wasn't trying to be a science textbook

When Genesis 1 describes God making a "firmament" (Hebrew *raqia*), a solid dome that holds
back waters above the sky, it's describing the universe the way everyone in the ancient Near
East pictured it. God spoke to people inside the picture of the world they already had.

That's not a flaw you have to explain away. It's a clue about what kind of writing this is.
Genesis 1 is a carefully built, poetic claim about *who* made the world and *why*: that it's a
gift, not a battlefield; that every human carries God's image; that the whole thing is
headed for rest. Reading it as a science report is reading it badly, not faithfully.

## Different questions

Science is superb at answering "how does this work?" and "what happens if?" It has no tools to
answer "why is there anything at all?", "is anything actually right or wrong?", or "does my
life mean something?" Those aren't unscientific questions. They're just not scientific ones.

You don't have to choose between your biology class and Jesus. Many Christians accept
evolution as the way God made life. Some don't. Following Jesus doesn't hang on it.

## Where this leaves you

If science is the reason you set faith aside, it's worth asking whether you set aside the real
thing or a caricature. You can keep every bit of your respect for evidence and still ask the
questions science can't answer. Jesus never asked anyone to stop thinking. He asked them to
follow him and see.
