Maybe you'd like to believe, but you can't make the questions go away. Maybe you used to be
sure and now you're not. Maybe you think Christians are people who've stopped asking, and you
could never be one.

Here's the good news: you don't have to be certain to start.

## Doubt is all over the Bible

- A desperate father asks Jesus to heal his son and blurts out, "I believe; help my unbelief!"
  (Mark 9:24). Jesus heals the boy. He doesn't demand a cleaner answer first.
- After the resurrection, the disciples meet Jesus on a mountain. Matthew says, "they
  worshiped him, but some doubted" (Matthew 28:17). Jesus goes on to give them all the same
  commission, doubters included.
- Thomas refuses to believe until he can see and touch. Jesus doesn't send him away. He shows
  up and offers exactly what Thomas asked for (John 20:24–29).
- The Psalms are full of people asking "How long?" and "Where are you?" and "Why?"

Doubt isn't a failure of faith in these stories. It's part of how faith works.

## What "faith" actually means

We tend to hear "faith" as "believing something without evidence" or "being certain." The
Greek word in the New Testament, *pistis*, is closer to trust, faithfulness, and loyalty. It's
the kind of word you'd use for a friend who's reliable, or a soldier who's loyal to a king.

That changes the question. It's not "am I sure enough?" It's "am I willing to trust him, and
follow him, with the questions I still have?"

## Even the saints doubted

Mother Teresa, whose name is nearly shorthand for faith, wrote in private letters about decades
of feeling God's absence. She kept praying and kept serving the dying in Calcutta. That's not
the absence of faith. That's what it can look like.

## Where this leaves you

You can bring your doubts with you. Write them down, plainly and specifically, not to solve
them but to see them. Then take one small step anyway: read a chapter of Mark, or say one honest
sentence to God. People have followed Jesus for two thousand years with questions in their
pockets. You'd be in very good company.
