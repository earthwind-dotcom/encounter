If a church hurt you, you're not being dramatic and you're not being "bitter." Maybe you were
shamed for asking questions. Maybe someone used God's name to control you. Maybe something much
worse happened, and people protected the institution instead of you.

First: if you've been abused, what happened was wrong, and it isn't your job to fix it or to
forgive on anyone else's timeline. Please talk to someone you trust, and if a crime was
committed, you can report it. None of what follows is meant to hurry you.

## We won't defend it

The history is real. Churches have covered up abuse. They've blessed racism and violence.
They've used fear and guilt to keep people in line. Pretending otherwise would be one more
dishonesty, and you've probably had enough of those.

## Jesus was on your side of this

Here's something worth knowing. The people Jesus was angriest with weren't the ones the
religious world looked down on. They were the religious leaders. He called them "whitewashed
tombs," clean on the outside and rotten inside. He said they tied heavy burdens on people's
backs and wouldn't lift a finger to help. He said they shut the door of God's kingdom in
people's faces (Matthew 23).

If you've been on the receiving end of that, Jesus saw it, named it, and opposed it. He's not
the one who hurt you.

## The church is people, and people fail

The Bible doesn't pretend its heroes were good. David, Israel's greatest king, committed
adultery and arranged a murder. Peter, Jesus' closest friend, denied knowing him three times.
The New Testament letters are full of churches getting it wrong. It's a book that tells the
truth about religious people.

That's not an excuse for anyone who harmed you. It's a reason not to confuse the people who
misused Jesus' name with Jesus himself.

## If you ever do come back

You don't have to. But if you ever want to try a community again, here are some things a
healthy one does:

- It tells the truth, including about its own failures.
- It welcomes questions and doubt instead of punishing them.
- Its leaders are accountable to someone, and nobody is above being questioned.
- It lets you say no, and doesn't punish you for it.
- It never asks you to keep a secret that protects someone powerful.

## Where this leaves you

You can come close to Jesus without walking into a building. Read about him on your own. Talk
to him honestly, including about what the church did. Take as long as you need. If and when you
want to talk to a real person about any of this, [you can](/talk), and you can stop the
conversation at any time.
