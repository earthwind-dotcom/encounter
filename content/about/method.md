## The sequence

Every passage runs through the same inductive exegetical procedure before anything is written
for publication.

**Observation first,** with no interpretation allowed: read the passage and its book, write down
every question it raises, look for patterns and contrasts, compare translations and note where
they diverge. **Then the text gets established:** where the passage starts and stops, what kind of
writing it is, how it's built. **Then the digging:** key words, a full word study through the
standard lexicons, the historical situation, the cultural background. **Then it lands:** check
three or more critical commentaries, state the main idea in one sentence, and draw out what it
says about God and about us.

A sermon in Reflections, a study in Roots or Provenance, a course session and a Hard Question on
the same passage share the same underlying work.

## The three rules

**Never invent a citation.** Every reference is checked against publisher, library or journal
records. Anything that can't be confirmed is dropped rather than softened.

**Label the confidence.** Every substantive claim carries one of five labels, so you always know
whether you're being handed settled ground or a judgement call:

- [consensus] Nearly all specialists agree, believing or not.
- [strong majority] Most agree; a real minority dissents.
- [debated] The field is genuinely split.
- [our read] Our judgement, offered as ours.
- [rejected] A popular claim the evidence doesn't support.

**State the counter-view.** Where serious scholars disagree, the disagreement is printed, named,
and given its best form. That includes skeptics.

## What counts as a source

Critical commentaries, the standard lexicons and dictionaries, critical editions, and
peer-reviewed work carry the weight. Popular books are read and recommended, but they aren't
load-bearing. Where a claim rests on one scholar's reconstruction rather than a field consensus,
that's said out loud.

## Corrections

If something here is wrong, it should be fixed, not defended. A site that labels its own
confidence has no business being precious about a mistake. Tell us through
[Talk to someone](/talk); a real person reads every message. Corrections are listed on the
[status page](/about/status).

## A note on the voice

Plain language, contractions, no em dashes, and no church word left untranslated. Where one is
unavoidable, the plain version comes first. Clarity is treated as a form of respect, not a
stylistic preference.
