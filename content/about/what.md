## The premise

Most people in our part of the world think they already know what Christianity is. They met a
version of Jesus secondhand: from a church they left, a culture war, a movie, a meme. Usually
it's a version worth walking away from.

Encounter exists to introduce the real one. The Jesus of the first-century sources, read closely,
with the best scholarship we have, in plain words.

People are usually handed two options. Either the Bible is a flat, seamless book and any
difficulty is a threat to be argued away, or it's a pile of contradictions and the honest move
is to walk. Both options ask you to stop looking. Encounter is built on the belief that there's a
third thing, and that it's better than either: read the text closely, say what scholars actually
know and where they actually disagree, and let the faith stand on its own feet without being
propped up by claims that won't hold.

We don't defend the Bible by quoting the Bible at you. We show you the evidence, tell you how
sure anyone can be, and let you weigh it.

## What we hope happens

We'll say it plainly, because hiding it would be dishonest: we hope you'll come to trust Jesus,
follow him, and find a community of people doing the same. That's the point.

We also want you to get there honestly or not at all. So there's no pressure here, no fear
tactics, no bait-and-switch, and no counting "decisions." You're free to say no, or not yet, and
keep coming.

## What's here

- **Hard Questions.** Straight answers to the things that stop people: whether Jesus existed,
  whether the resurrection happened, suffering, hell, science, the Bible's contradictions, the
  church's record.
- **The Course.** Thirteen sessions, from where the documents came from to what a life following
  Jesus looks like. Alone, with a friend, or at a table with a group.
- **The Library.** The research underneath everything else: sermons printed with their working
  shown (Reflections), word studies (Roots), and where the texts came from (Provenance).
- **Talk to someone.** A real person reads every message.

## Who makes this

Encounter is written by Irwin Ortega. The method it runs on is the inductive exegetical procedure
taught in the biblical exposition and exegesis practicum sequence at Life Pacific University,
worked through for every passage before a word of the public piece is written.

That's the whole of the qualification, and it's stated plainly for the same reason everything
else is: so you can weigh it. The scholarship Encounter leans on isn't ours. It's named on every
page, and where the people who produced it disagree with each other, the disagreement is printed
rather than quietly resolved in our favour.

The tone owes a lot to *The Bible for Normal People* and the research to scholars across the
spectrum, including the BibleProject and skeptics like Bart Ehrman, whose best arguments are
presented fairly.

## Where the name came from

This started as **Marginalia**: the notes readers write in the margins, the questions and
objections and places somebody stopped and argued with the page. That posture hasn't changed.
What changed is the aim. The notes were always pointing at someone. **Encounter** is about meeting
him.
