audience: facilitator

The plain-language presentation facilitators use, plus how to invite a response and how to
pray with someone. No jargon (see Language Bridge). Honest, not fear-based, not
pressured. The goal is a followed life, not a prayer said once.

## The message, in five plain moves

Say it as a conversation, not a script. Ten minutes, not a talk.

1. **You were made for connection.** With God, with other people, with your own life. When
   it's working, you know it. There's a way things are supposed to be.
2. **Something's broken.** The world, and us. Everyone feels the gap between how things
   should be and how they are, and the gap between who you want to be and who you actually
   are. The old word for our part in it is "sin." Plainly: the pull to run your own life
   your own way, and the wreckage that follows, in you and around you.
3. **God didn't stay at a distance.** Jesus is God stepping into it in person. He lived the
   life. At the cross he took the wreckage onto himself instead of leaving it on us. The
   resurrection is the sign that it worked, and that death is not the last word.
4. **The offer.** You don't clean yourself up first. You come as you are. You trust him,
   and you start following him. He does the changing, from the inside, over time, with
   other people around you. It's not a self-improvement project you run alone.
5. **The response.** Three things, and you can do all three in a sentence: turn toward him
   (a change of direction), trust him (lean your weight on it), and start following. In
   your own words, out loud or silent, right now or later.

## Inviting a response

Plain, once, without pressure. Something like:

> "I'm going to say straight out what this is heading toward. It's following Jesus. Not
> signing off on a list of beliefs. Trusting a person, and letting him lead your life.
>
> You don't have to do anything tonight. If you want to, it isn't complicated. You tell
> him, in your own words, that you're in. If you're not there, that's a real answer, and
> you're welcome here as long as you want to keep coming."

Then stop. Let it sit. Don't fill the silence.

## Praying with someone

- **Ask first.** "Would you like to pray about that now, or think about it?"
- Let them use **their own words**. If they'd rather, give them a line at a time to repeat.
- Keep it **short**. A sample, for someone who wants one:

  > "Jesus, I don't have it all figured out. I think you're real, and worth trusting. I'm
  > sorry for running my life my own way. Thank you for taking the weight of that. I want
  > to follow you. Help me."

- Afterward, tell them plainly what's true: you belong to him now, this is the start and
  not the finish, and nobody expects you to have it together. Then connect them to the next
  real thing: a practice from the course, a person,
  Stage 2 — Practicing, and eventually Stage 3 — Threshold & Continuing.

## What not to do

- No "every head bowed, every eye closed." No spotlight, no hands raised.
- No emotional pressure, no repeating "are you sure you don't want to?"
- Never imply someone is in danger if they leave the room without praying.
- Don't rush a person who's wrestling. Wrestling is a good sign, not a problem to fix.
- Don't perform it, and don't treat the prayer as the finish line. A prayer with no
  community, no practice, and no follow-up has led nobody anywhere.
