**The question**
Can these accounts be trusted at all?

**The text: Luke 1:1–4**
> Since many have undertaken to set down an orderly account of the events that have been
> fulfilled among us, just as they were handed on to us by those who from the beginning
> were eyewitnesses and servants of the word, I too decided, after investigating everything
> carefully from the very first, to write an orderly account for you, most excellent
> Theophilus, so that you may know the truth concerning the things about which you have been  instructed. *(NRSV)*

*Kind of writing: the formal opening of an ancient historical work, addressed to a patron.*

**What we know**
- The four Gospels were written roughly 40 to 70 years after Jesus' death, between about 70 and 100 CE. Mark is usually dated first. Paul's letters are earlier, from the 50s. *[consensus]*
- They circulated at first without author names. The titles "according to Matthew / Mark / Luke / John" appear in the second century; Irenaeus names all four around 180 CE. *[consensus that the titles are later; debated whether the traditional names are accurate]*
- We have over 5,000 Greek manuscripts, plus translations and quotations. They differ in thousands of small ways (spelling, word order) and a few larger ones (the end of Mark, the woman caught in adultery), all flagged in modern Bibles. No teaching of the church rests on a disputed reading. *[consensus]*
- "Eyewitness" and "orderly account" in Luke's world meant inherited material, arranged and shaped for a purpose. Looser than a court transcript, tighter than legend. *[one major view]*

**Try this**
Read Mark chapter 1 tonight as if you had never heard any of it. Write three or four
sentences of honest first impression. Keep it.

**For the table**
1. What does Luke actually claim to be doing in verses 1–4?
2. Does knowing these were written decades later, by people arranging inherited material, make you trust them more or less? Why?
3. What would it take for you to consider a document like this reliable?

**One next step**
Your written first impression of Mark 1. Bring it back next week.

**Driving question.** For someone with no church background this is the gate. If the
documents are late legends, nothing else in the course matters. The honest answer is more
interesting than either "inerrant" or "made up."

**Run of show.** Meal 20 · Question 5 · Text 15 · Brief 20 · Practice 10 · Table 30 ·
Invitation 2 · Close 3.

**The text, how to read it.** Luke 1:1–4 is one long, careful Greek sentence, the kind an
ancient writer used to open a serious work. Read it aloud. Notice: "many have undertaken"
(Luke knows he isn't first), "handed on to us" (inherited material), "investigating
everything carefully" (he claims research), "an orderly account" (he is arranging, not
just recording), "most excellent Theophilus" (a dedication to a patron). Ask the group
what kind of document opens like this.

**The brief, say roughly this.**

1. *Timing.* Jesus was executed around 30 CE. The Gospels were written down between about
   70 and 100 CE, Mark first. That's within living memory, but after the first generation
   began to die, which is part of why they got written. *[consensus]*
2. *The names.* The Gospels do not name their authors in the text. The familiar titles turn
   up in the second century; Irenaeus, around 180, is the first to name all four and argue
   there must be exactly four. Whether the traditional names are the real authors is
   debated. That the titles are later is not. *[consensus / debated]*
3. *The copies.* Over 5,000 Greek manuscripts, plus translations and quotations in early
   writers. They disagree in thousands of places, nearly all trivial. A handful of larger
   passages are disputed and marked in every modern Bible (Mark 16:9–20; John 7:53–8:11).
   The discipline that sorts this is textual criticism, and its results are public. No
   doctrine hangs on a contested verse. *[consensus]*
4. *What "reliable" meant then.* Ancient historians, Luke included, arranged material to
   make a point, composed speeches, smoothed chronology. That does not make them liars; it
   makes them ancient writers. The question is not "is every detail a video transcript" but
   "are they giving us the substance of what happened and what it meant." *[one major view]*

Introduce the three confidence labels here and use them all course.

**The practice, how to introduce it.** "Before we study Mark to death, read it once like a
stranger. Chapter 1, tonight, no commentary. Write down your gut reaction, whatever it is.
We come back to it at the end of the course and see if it moved." The point is an honestly
recorded baseline.

**Likely objections.**
- *"Written that late, it's all Chinese whispers."* Concede the gap is real. Then: this is oral transmission in a culture built for it, with named people still alive to correct it, and Paul's letters from the 50s already carry the core claims. Not a game of telephone; a community's shared memory getting written down before the witnesses were gone.
- *"The church chose the books, so it's rigged."* The four Gospels were functioning as authoritative well before any council voted; the early lists mostly ratify what was already in use. Flag it for a fuller session later.
- *"Scholars just want to tear it down."* The people quoted here range from devout to atheist and agree on most of this. Disagreement starts later, on interpretation.

**Facilitation notes.**
- Hard for people from a strict-inerrancy background; they can hear this as an attack. Name it: "this isn't demolishing anything, it's telling you what you're actually holding."
- Do not oversell certainty or undersell it. The measured answer is the credible one.
- Let the silence after the first objection sit. People need a second to decide you are not dodging.
